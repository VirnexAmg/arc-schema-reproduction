# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- The controllable object is the 5x5 color-12/9 payload. Every observed translation preserves its two-row color-12 cap and three-row color-9 body.
- The asymmetric blue/black glyph remains fixed in the left room and may be an instruction or target pattern.
- The framed upper glyph has a lower socket at payload top row 15. ACTION1 cannot enter its patterned center, while ACTION2 undocks the payload downward.
- The bottom color-11 strip is an action meter. Every successful observed translation consumes its leftmost remaining cell on both rows; blocked motion consumes nothing.
- Motion is quantized into five-pixel cells. Color 4 blocks motion; colors 3, 5, and 9 can lie under the payload.
- The payload is now at top row 25, column 34, in a forty-pixel-wide horizontal color-3 corridor. Both five-pixel lateral destinations are visibly open.

## Stable hypotheses
- H_directional_move: ACTION1 moves up five pixels and ACTION2 moves down five pixels when the destination is traversable.
- H_action2_displace is supported by two consecutive downward moves.
- H_underlay_restore is supported: successful motion restores the exact static terrain beneath the vacated footprint.
- H_dock_target: top row 15 is an upper docking position that blocks ACTION1 but can be exited with ACTION2.
- H_action3_left and H_action3_right are competing mappings for the untried ACTION3 at the open corridor.

## Evidence
- Transitions 1-6: ACTION1 moved the payload from top row 45 to row 15 and advanced the meter once per successful move.
- Transition 7: ACTION1 at row 15 was an exact blocked no-op.
- Transitions 8-9: ACTION2 moved downward from row 15 to row 20 and then row 25. Each move preserved the payload, restored the vacated terrain, and advanced the meter once.
- The second ACTION2 confirms repeatable directional control rather than a socket-specific release effect.
