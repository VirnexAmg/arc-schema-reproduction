# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- The movable 5x5 magenta/dark-red player is at x=24..28, y=30..34, immediately right of the tiny black/blue corridor glyph.
- Actions are tile moves: 1 up, 2 down, 3 left, 4 right; each tile is 5 pixels.
- Color 3 is the main corridor; color 5 is enterable socket floor. The dark-red top glyph is presently solid.
- The tiny black/blue glyph near x=17..19, y=31..33 is a distinct interaction object.
- The large lower-left glyph is a status indicator. Approaching the tiny glyph changed it by erasing a horizontal stroke.
- The bottom bar advances each action and is likely a move/time budget.

## Stable hypotheses
- H_navigation: directional movement is established, subject to walls.
- H_target / H_socket_navigation: the top socket is the eventual goal but its glyph blocks entry until another interaction changes state.
- H_key_unlock: approaching the tiny glyph activates a switch and changes the status glyph; this likely unlocks or transforms the top target.

## Experiments and falsifications
- Entering the socket at y=15 did not complete the level.
- A second upward action there was blocked, falsifying direct deeper traversal.
- The prior model incorrectly predicted that downward movement onto the tiny-glyph row would be blocked. Observation showed the player moved onto y=30 and the lower-left status glyph changed. The model now treats colors 0/1 as traversable interaction pixels and models proximity activation approximately.
- Moving right after activation moved away normally; the tiny glyph remains visually present, so activation is represented by the separate status display rather than collection/removal.

## Current plan
- Return to the top socket after activation and test whether the target is now passable or completes on contact. The predictive model is certified for navigation, with three small visual approximations.
