# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Main playfield is a 5-pixel lattice maze: color 3 is traversable corridor, color 4 is wall/exterior.
- Movable 5x5 token has color 12 in its top two rows and color 9 in its bottom three.
- Rows 61-62 hold a move/progress meter: legal ordinary moves convert the leftmost 11 column to 3.
- Embedded 0/1 glyph at the left branch is a reusable input cell.
- Framed color-5/9 pattern near x=32..40,y=8..16 is a target/display glyph, not directly enterable from below.
- Lower HUD rows 55-60 encode the current input-pattern phase.

## Established mechanisms
- ACTION1=up, ACTION2=down, ACTION3=left, ACTION4=right.
- Legal moves shift the token by one 5-pixel lattice cell, except corridor geometry can visually align it within a narrowing branch.
- Destination cannot contain color 4 and must contain corridor 3 or an embedded 0/1 marker.
- Vacated input cell restores its 0/1 glyph.
- Ordinary legal moves advance the rows 61-62 meter.
- First observed input entry changes HUD rows 57-58 without advancing meter. Second entry changes rows 55-56 and 59-60 and advances the meter. Full timeline backtest passes this phase model.
- Direct upward movement into the framed upper glyph is blocked; moving back down is legal.

## Competing mechanism hypotheses
- H1: repeated glyph entry cycles the lower HUD until its pattern matches the framed upper target, then the level completes or opens an exit. This is currently strongest.
- H2: the glyph is one of multiple maze inputs, and exploration must locate further glyphs.
- H3: matching the target requires choosing entry direction or another operation, rather than merely repeated entry.

## Falsified hypotheses
- Input glyph is a one-time collectible: it reappears after leaving.
- Arbitrary color-5 regions are traversable floor: blocked upward evidence rules this out.
- The framed upper glyph is directly enterable from the vertical corridor: upward action at its boundary produced no change.

## Needs testing
- Return from the upper branch to the reusable input glyph and enter it again.
- Observe whether another activation changes the HUD, completes the level, or opens a route.
- Generalize latent-marker restoration and HUD phase cycling without level-specific coordinates once more levels provide evidence.
