# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Main playfield is a 5-pixel lattice maze (3=floor, 4=wall); token is a 5x5 object with two color-12 rows above three color-9 rows.
- Rows 61-62 contain the 42-step attempt strip and three status lamps.
- Level 1 had a reusable 0/1 glyph, framed terminal, target glyph, and 3x3 register.
- Level 2 has a framed 9-pattern around x12..20,y38..46; a 0/1 glyph around x49..51,y46..48; a lower 11 glyph around x39..43,y51..53; and an upper 11 glyph around x14..18,y16..18.
- Current token is at lattice cell (14,10). ACTION2 would move it onto the cell at (14,15), whose footprint contains the upper 11 glyph.

## Established mechanisms
- ACTION1=up, ACTION2=down, ACTION3=left, ACTION4=right.
- Legal movement shifts one 5-pixel lattice cell; ordinary collisions generally consume an attempt.
- Attempt-strip exhaustion triggers checkpoint reset on the following action.
- On level 1, activating the input glyph transformed the register; returning to the framed terminal with register 111/001/101 and pressing ACTION1 submitted it and completed the level.
- Level-2 directional movement follows the same action mapping.

## Mechanism hypotheses
- H1: Embedded glyph cells are operators; entering one transforms puzzle state.
- H2: The upper and lower 11 glyphs are reusable checkpoint operators that refill the attempt strip without changing the register.
- H3: The upper 11 glyph is merely passable/decorative and entry consumes the normal two meter columns.
- H4: Framing distinguishes terminal/target/operator roles; the framed 9-pattern is likely a terminal or target, while raw 11 and 0/1 glyphs are operators.

## Ruled out
- Framed-terminal upward action is not always inert: matching the target completed level 1.
- Level coordinates/routes do not remain fixed after level-up.
