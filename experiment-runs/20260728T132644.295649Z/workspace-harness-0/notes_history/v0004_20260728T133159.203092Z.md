# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Main playfield is a 5-pixel lattice maze: color 3 is traversable corridor, color 4 is wall/exterior.
- Movable 5x5 token has color 12 in its top two rows and color 9 in its bottom three.
- Rows 61-62 hold a move/progress meter: each ordinary legal move converts the leftmost 11 column to 3.
- Embedded 0/1 glyph footprints are enterable special cells.
- The framed color-5 pattern near x=32..40,y=8..16 is decorative/non-enterable in the currently observed state.
- Lower HUD rows 55-60 encode several two-pixel-wide bit/glyph segments.

## Established mechanisms
- ACTION1=up, ACTION2=down, ACTION3=left; ACTION4=right is strongly implied but not directly observed.
- Legal moves shift the token by one 5-pixel lattice cell. A destination is legal if wall-free and either contains corridor color 3 or a 0/1 glyph.
- Vacated token pixels restore underlying corridor or glyph.
- Ordinary legal moves advance the rows 61-62 meter.
- Entering the glyph at lattice cell x=20,y=30 does not advance that meter and changes lower HUD bits.

## Revised interpretation
- The glyph is not consumed: after leaving it downward, its 0/1 shape reappeared, and entering it again changed additional HUD rows/segments. It is therefore likely a reusable switch/input rather than a collectible.
- The upper framed 9-pixel glyph may be a target pattern, while lower HUD pixels encode current switch state, a sequence, or multiple channels.

## Falsified hypotheses
- Special glyph as a one-time collectible is ruled out by its restoration after departure.
- Arbitrary color-5 regions are not enterable; blocked upward transition established this.

## Needs testing
- Directly test ACTION4=right.
- Explore maze for other glyph switches and determine whether switch activation cycles/toggles a local HUD state.
- Determine relation between upper target glyph and lower HUD, and exact completion condition.
