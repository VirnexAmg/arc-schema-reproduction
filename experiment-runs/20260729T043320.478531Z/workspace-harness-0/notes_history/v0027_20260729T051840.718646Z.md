# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Yellow (4) is wall/background; green (3) and gray (5) form traversable corridors/chambers.
- Avatar is a 5x5 block with two color-12 rows over three color-9 rows.
- The small blue/black glyph in the maze is a directional switch; the upper red glyph is a lock whose 3x3 pattern is the target combination.
- The bottom-left 10x6 panel is a doubled-pixel 3x3 switch/FSM display. The cyan strip in rows 61..62 is the remaining ordinary-input budget.

## Mechanism hypotheses
- Recorded controls: ACTION1 up, ACTION2 down, ACTION3 left, ACTION4 right, in 5-cell increments.
- Switch contacts update the 3x3 display rather than simply collecting the glyph.
- Critical visual grounding: the current display decodes to `#.# / ..# / ###`, exactly matching the upper lock's red 3x3 glyph. This is likely the completed combination.
- Therefore the next objective is reusable: make the HUD pattern equal the lock glyph, then navigate to the upper chamber and press toward the lock.
- Current avatar is on the left side of the central wall. Route to the lock should go down through the left notch, right along the bottom corridor, then up the right side and central stem.
- Timeout restores avatar/HUD/budget but preserves some latent switch phase.
- Ordinary successful moves and ordinary collisions spend one cyan column. Recognized switch/checkpoint transitions and upper-lock attempts can be free.
