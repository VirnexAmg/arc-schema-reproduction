# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Yellow (4) is wall/background; green (3) and gray (5) form traversable corridors/chambers.
- Avatar is a 5x5 block with two color-12 rows over three color-9 rows.
- The small blue/black glyph in the maze is a directional switch; the upper red/black glyph is a locked goal/door.
- The bottom-left 10x6 panel is the switch/FSM display. The cyan strip in rows 61..62 is the remaining ordinary-input budget.

## Mechanism hypotheses
- Recorded controls: ACTION1 up, ACTION2 down, ACTION3 left, ACTION4 right, in 5-cell increments.
- Approaching the central switch from different directions updates different parts of the 10x6 HUD glyph.
- Rightward contacts count through a horizontal sub-sequence; the third contact is a free recognized transition. Later contacts cost meter again.
- A leftward contact after the three-right latch opens a persistent reversal phase. The first such left contact keeps the horizontal projection, while a subsequent left contact in the already-open reversal phase restores the canonical glyph (top solid bar, left middle pair, split bottom).
- Vertical entries select/retain the middle pair according to FSM phase. Timeout restores avatar/HUD/budget but preserves some latent contact/reversal phase.
- Ordinary successful moves and ordinary collisions spend one cyan column. Recognized switch/checkpoint transitions and upper-lock attempts can be free.

## Falsified / ruled out
- Immediate adjacency to the upper glyph completes the level: false.
- Meter advances iff movement succeeds: false.
- ACTION3 and ACTION4 are identical: false.
- Three right contacts alone unlock the door: false.
- Timeout clears all FSM state: false.
- Horizontal contacts always overwrite all six HUD rows: false; historical step 23 requires preservation of the existing middle pair.
- The latest ACTION3 mismatch falsified the rule that every late left contact merely projects horizontal outer rows. Replaying all 104 transitions supports dependence on an already-open horizontal_reversed phase: a subsequent left contact restores the canonical six-row glyph.
- A simple right-contact-count threshold (>=5) for that restoration is unsupported and failed replay.
