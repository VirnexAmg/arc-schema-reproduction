# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Yellow (4) is wall/background; green (3) and gray (5) form corridors/chambers.
- Avatar is a 5x5 block with two color-12 rows over three color-9 rows; currently at x15,y30 beside the central blue/black directional switch.
- Upper red/black glyph is a locked goal/door.
- Bottom-left 10x6 panel records directional switch/FSM state; cyan (11) in rows 61..62 is the remaining input budget.

## Mechanism hypotheses
- ACTION1/2/3/4 map to up/down/left/right in 5-cell increments.
- Directional contacts with the central glyph update separate rows of the HUD as an FSM: horizontal approaches affect outer rows and a middle phase; vertical approaches select the middle pair.
- Three rightward contacts complete and latch a horizontal sub-sequence; the third is free, while later contacts cost meter.
- After horizontal latching, vertical traversal preserves the outer rows but still records direction in rows 57..58: ACTION1 selects the right pair and ACTION2 selects the left pair.
- Timeout restores avatar and visible HUD but preserves corridor-entry/contact phase.
- The HUD likely encodes a longer directional sequence that unlocks the upper door.
- Ordinary inputs spend one cyan column, including ordinary wall collisions; recognized FSM/checkpoint transitions and upper-lock interaction can be free.

## Falsified / ruled out
- Immediate adjacency to upper glyph completes the level: false.
- Meter advances iff movement succeeds: false.
- ACTION3 and ACTION4 are identical: false.
- Three rightward switch contacts alone unlock the upper door: false.
- Timeout clears all FSM state: false.
- ACTION3 horizontal contact is disabled once symbol_activated is true: false.
- After right_contacts >= 3, downward corridor re-entry leaves the entire HUD unchanged: false. Latest ACTION2 changed rows 57..58 from the right pair to the left pair while preserving outer rows.
