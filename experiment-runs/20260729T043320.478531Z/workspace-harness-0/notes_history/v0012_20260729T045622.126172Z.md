# Working notes

## Grounded objects
- Yellow (4) is wall/background; green (3) and gray (5) form corridors/chambers.
- Avatar is a 5x5 block: two color-12 rows above three color-9 rows; current origin is x24,y45.
- Blue/black glyph near x16..18,y31..33 is a directional floor switch.
- Upper red/black glyph is a locked goal/door.
- Bottom-left panel is a directional/status HUD. Cyan (11) in rows 61..62 is a 42-input budget.

## Mechanism hypotheses
- Recorded mapping: ACTION1 up, ACTION2 down, ACTION3 left, ACTION4 right, in 5-cell increments.
- Green/gray and blue/black switch cells are traversable; yellow and the unsatisfied upper lock are solid.
- The switch responds to a particular approach direction; right-to-left (ACTION3) is implicated.
- HUD likely requests or records a directional sequence at the switch; satisfying it may unlock the upper door.
- Ordinary inputs spend one cyan column, including collisions with ordinary maze walls. Special interactions (lower-corridor entry, red checkpoint collection, and blocked upward interaction at the upper lock) can be free.
- If cyan is already empty, the next input is swallowed and causes an in-level reset to the lower red checkpoint, restoring HUD and meter without GAME_OVER.

## Falsified / ruled out
- Immediate adjacency to the upper red glyph completes the level: false.
- Meter advances iff movement succeeds: false; blocked ACTION2 and the newest blocked ACTION1 at the central wall spend ticks.
- All blocked ACTION1 inputs are free: false. The observed free case is spatially specific to the upper-lock interaction.
- Touching the switch from any direction necessarily activates it: false; approach direction matters.
- Meter exhaustion immediately emits GAME_OVER: false.
- Timeout input performs an ordinary move or reset-then-move: false; it is swallowed.
