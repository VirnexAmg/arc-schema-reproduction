# Working notes

## Objects
- 64x64 scene with a 5x5 moving token (two color-12 rows above three color-9 rows), currently at x=19..23,y=30..34.
- Static terrain uses colors 3/4/5. Ordinary traversable route tiles are color 3 on a 5-pixel lattice.
- Rows 61..62 are a move meter: most effective moves and ordinary wall collisions replace the leftmost color-11 column with color 3.
- Rows 53..60 contain a multi-mark status display made from color 9 on color 5.
- The patterned chamber centered near y=8..19 contains an upper docking tile at x=34,y=15.
- A small color-0/1 glyph was embedded in the route tile x=19,y=30.

## Established mechanisms
- ACTION1/2/3 are up/down/left by one 5-pixel tile. ACTION4 is predicted right but untested.
- Entry succeeds on all-color-3 tiles, on embedded glyph tiles containing only 0/1/3, and on the special upper dock.
- Vacated tiles become route color 3; entering a glyph therefore consumes it.
- Ordinary successful movement advances the move meter. Entering the observed glyph did not advance that meter; instead status marks at x=3..4,y=57..58 extinguished while x=7..8,y=57..58 relit.
- Ordinary blocked moves consume meter. ACTION1 while already docked at y=15 is an exceptional total no-op.

## Experiments and evidence
- Cardinal movement/collision model passes all 21 transitions.
- ACTION3 from x=24,y=30 entered the color-0/1 glyph tile rather than being blocked, proving glyphs are traversable interactions.
- The glyph changed the lower status display exactly as described above and was erased beneath the token.
- Docking in the upper chamber before collecting the glyph did not complete the level.

## Competing hypotheses
- The glyph changes a key/state that must be carried back to the upper terminal; status marks visualize that state.
- Alternatively, several glyphs or destinations must be visited in sequence through the lower maze before returning.
